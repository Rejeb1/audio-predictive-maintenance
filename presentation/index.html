A failing machine often sounds different first

Worn bearings, leaks and imbalance change a machine's acoustic signature. Today, spotting this relies on experienced technicians or costly vibration sensors.

Question: can we detect anomalies from plain audio, learning only from normal sounds, despite factory noise?

Three ways to model normal sound

I

Dense autoencoder

Rebuilds 5 short frames at a time. Same design as the official DCASE baseline.

II

Convolutional autoencoder

Treats the spectrogram as an image and rebuilds time-frequency patches.

III

Pretrained embeddings

PANNs describes each clip; we measure the distance to normal clips.

5. Explainability and deploymentAymen Turki

What we learned, and what is missing

1

Findings

Normal sounds alone can reveal faults. The best method depends on the machine, and noise is a major limit. The larger models did not always win.

2

Limits

Most faults are missed at our threshold. Two machine types, one training run per model, and no other microphone or factory tested.

3

Next steps

One autoencoder per machine, scoring the most abnormal frames, self-supervision with machine IDs, and recalibration on site.
